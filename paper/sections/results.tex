\subsection{RQ1: per-token KV cost fell every year; per-parameter cost fell with a 2025 rebound}
\label{sec:rq1}

Table~\ref{tab:overview} and Figure~\ref{fig:kv-trend} summarize the deduplicated census of \kvcDedupRows{} geometry groups. The median marginal KV cost in BF16 fell in every release year: \kvcMedianKvTokTwentyThree{} bytes per token in 2023, \kvcMedianKvTokTwentyFour{} in 2024, \kvcMedianKvTokTwentyFive{} in 2025, and \kvcMedianKvTokTwentySix{} in 2026. Spearman rank correlations against release date confirm a decline on both outcomes: $\rho=\kvcSpearmanRho$ ($p=\kvcSpearmanP$) for KV bytes per token and $\rho=\kvcSpearmanRhoPP$ ($p=\kvcSpearmanPPp$) for KV per parameter. The correlations are weak in magnitude because the within-year spread dwarfs the between-year shift; in Figure~\ref{fig:kv-trend} the full-attention, windowed, recurrent/hybrid, and MLA-latent classes overlap across orders of magnitude at every date. The decline tracks the diffusion of cost-saving designs rather than a single event: KV-head reduction cuts the per-token cost at fixed width \cite{shazeer2019mqa,ainslie2023gqa}, sliding-window layers bound the cache \cite{jiang2023mistral}, and latent compression shrinks it further \cite{deepseekai2024v2}; Figure~\ref{fig:kv-trend} shows windowed and recurrent/hybrid points becoming denser at later dates.

The per-parameter picture depends on the counting unit, and we report all of them. Under the pre-registered checkpoint-family unit, median KV bytes per token per $10^{9}$ parameters dropped from \kvcMedianKvppTwentyThree{} in 2023 to \kvcMedianKvppTwentyFour{} in 2024, rebounded to \kvcMedianKvppTwentyFive{} in 2025---above the 2023 level---and fell to \kvcMedianKvppTwentySix{} in 2026. The rebound is an artifact of duplicated geometry, not an architectural regression: the \kvcDedupRows{} family-level groups realize only \kvcGeoRows{} distinct attention geometries, because same-geometry fine-tunes with distinct names survive family-level deduplication (worst case: \kvcWorstGeoCluster{} rows of a single 2023-era geometry, \kvcWorstGeoClusterRecent{} of them released in 2025 or later). Under one-row-per-geometry deduplication the series is monotone: \kvcMedianKvppGeoTwentyThree, \kvcMedianKvppGeoTwentyFour, \kvcMedianKvppGeoTwentyFive, \kvcMedianKvppGeoTwentySix{} (Table~\ref{tab:overview}), and Spearman $\rho$ strengthens to \kvcSpearmanRhoGeo{} ($p=\kvcSpearmanPGeo$).

Against the pre-registered threshold, H1 is \kvcHOneVerdict{} on the pre-registered unit: the pooled 2025--2026 median KV-per-parameter cost is \kvcHOneRatio{}$\times$ the 2023 median. Under geometry deduplication the ratio is \kvcHOneRatioGeo, also below the registered support level of $0.75$. One robustness cut reverses the verdict: multimodal checkpoints (\kvcMMRows{} of the dedup set) carry parameter counts that include their vision towers while their KV accounting covers only the language backbone, deflating their per-parameter KV cost; excluding them yields a pooled ratio of \kvcHOneRatioNoMM, above $0.75$. H1 is therefore supported under the pre-registered analysis and under geometry deduplication, but is sensitive to how multimodal parameter counts are handled; we flag this rather than pick the flattering cut.

Figure~\ref{fig:kv-params} regresses $\log_{10}$ KV bytes per token on $\log_{10}$ total parameters. The slope is \kvcParamsSlope{} (95\% CI \kvcParamsSlopeLo--\kvcParamsSlopeHi) with $R^{2}=\kvcParamsRsq$ (95\% CI \kvcParamsRsqLo--\kvcParamsRsqHi, 1000 bootstrap resamples), so H2 is \kvcHTwoVerdict{} by the pre-registered criterion ($R^{2}<0.5$): parameters alone leave most variance unexplained. The same regression on one row per geometry gives $R^{2}=\kvcParamsRsqGeo$, so the weakness is not an artifact of duplicated geometries. Attention geometry alone---GQA ratio (attention heads over KV heads; imputed to 1 where KV heads are absent or equal to attention heads), head dimension, depth, window share, recurrent share, and the MLA and cross-layer-sharing indicators---predicts KV cost with $R^{2}=\kvcGeomRsq$; adding log parameters raises it to \kvcFullRsq{} (parameters alone: \kvcParRsq). Two caveats: the outcome is by construction an exact function of the geometry predictors, so the geometry $R^{2}$ is only informative as a contrast with parameter count, and the full model still adds \kvcFullRsq{} versus \kvcGeomRsq. The regression uses $N=\kvcRegN{}$ groups after excluding \kvcRegNoParam{} groups without parameter counts and \kvcRegZeroKV{} zero-marginal-KV groups, whose asymptotic marginal cost is zero because every cache-bearing layer is windowed or recurrent (\kvcRegOverlapBoth{} groups lack both).

\input{generated/table_mechanism}

Table~\ref{tab:mechanism} reports fully standardized coefficients. Every sign matches naive per-layer accounting: KV-head reduction through GQA ($\beta=\kvcBetaGqa$), latent compression ($\beta=\kvcBetaLatent$, consistent with \cite{deepseekai2024v2}), recurrent state replacing KV ($\beta=\kvcBetaRecurrentShare$), and windowed layers ($\beta=\kvcBetaWindowShare$) all lower predicted KV cost, while depth raises it ($\beta=\kvcBetaDepth$): a model that spends its budget on more layers buys KV with them. The cross-layer-sharing indicator is estimated from only \kvcSharedN{} positive observations and its coefficient ($\beta=\kvcBetaShared$) should not be read as evidence; the mechanism is grounded instead in the accounting itself \cite{sun2024yoco,wu2024cla}. Publisher behavior mirrors these mechanisms: Gemma~3 re-architected its attention layout specifically to curb KV growth by shortening local spans and thinning global layers \cite{gemmateam2025gemma3}. Because the predictors collinearly encode one architecture (VIFs up to \kvcVifParams{} for parameters and \kvcVifDepth{} for depth, Table~\ref{tab:mechanism}), we read the variance decomposition as the mechanism evidence, not the individual magnitudes.

MoE models carry far less KV per total parameter, confirming H3 (\kvcHThreeVerdict). Median KV per $10^{9}$ parameters is \kvcMedKvppDense{} for dense models ($N=\kvcDenseN$) against \kvcMedKvppMoe{} for MoE models ($N=\kvcMoeN$): a ratio of \kvcHThreeRatio, far below the registered $0.5$ threshold (Mann--Whitney $p=\kvcMoeP$; rank-biserial \kvcMoeRB{} for the MoE group, negative meaning MoE values rank lower). The ratio is \kvcHThreeRatioGeo{} under geometry deduplication. Experts add parameters without adding per-token attention cache \cite{jiang2024mixtral,deepseekai2024v2}, and recent open families ship dense and MoE variants on the same attention stack \cite{yang2025qwen3}. Sizing serving hardware by total parameter count therefore overstates the memory cost of sparse models.

We define the KV-parity context $T^{*}$ as the context length at which one sequence's KV bytes equal its BF16 weight bytes; beyond $T^{*}$ the cache, not the weights, dominates memory. Figure~\ref{fig:capacity} and Table~\ref{tab:capacity} give the capacity view under fixed budgets. Three regimes coexist. Small full-context models serve tens of thousands of KV tokens at 24\,GiB and hundreds of thousands at 128\,GiB. The largest recent open models enter the table only at 128\,GiB, several of them only once weights drop to 4-bit---\texttt{gpt-oss-120b} reaches 1.8M total KV tokens there---and \kvcInfeasibleBigModels{} dedup groups with known parameters do not fit 128\,GiB even at 4-bit weights once the modeled 6\,GiB runtime overhead is applied (\kvcWeightsAloneBig{} of them exceed the budget on weights alone). Concurrency tells the same story: at 128\,GiB with BF16 weights and KV, the median model that fits at all serves \kvcConcMedThirtyTwoK{} concurrent 32k contexts but only \kvcConcMedOneHundredTwentyEightK{} concurrent 128k contexts, and \kvcConcMedOneHundredTwentyEightKZeroShare\% of groups cannot hold even one 128k context. Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity} varies the runtime overhead across the pre-registered 0--12\,GiB grid; the full grid (budgets $\times$ weight precisions $\times$ KV precisions $\times$ overheads) is exported with the results. At the other extreme, \texttt{starcoder2-3b} never saturates: every layer is windowed, so its cache is bounded and total capacity is unlimited. How much precision buys depends on the weight share of the budget. \texttt{OLMo-7B} serves 11k tokens at 24\,GiB with BF16 weights and 24k with FP8 weights, because freed weight bytes flow straight into the KV pool; at 128\,GiB the same model gains little (224k versus 237k) because its budget is already KV-dominant. For the largest models, weight quantization \cite{frantar2022gptq} and KV quantization \cite{liu2024kivi,hooper2024kvquant} decide whether any cache fits at all. This is the regime PagedAttention was built for: KV memory, not parameters, limits batch size \cite{kwon2023paged}, and on unified-memory devices it decides how many long contexts coexist \cite{rajesh2025edgemem}.

The parity trend (Figure~\ref{fig:parity}) is the honest counterweight to the capacity headline. Median $T^{*}$ moved from \kvcParityMedTwentyThree{} tokens in 2023 to \kvcParityMedTwentyFour{} in 2024, fell back to \kvcParityMedTwentyFive{} in 2025---slightly below the 2023 median---and jumped to \kvcParityMedTwentySix{} in 2026. The share of models that are KV-dominant at 32k context ($T^{*}<32$k) fell from \kvcDominantShareTwentyThree\% in 2023 to \kvcDominantShareTwentySix\% in 2026, but not monotonically: it rebounded to \kvcDominantShareTwentyFive\% in 2025, driven by the same 2025 cohort composition as in RQ1. A small and growing fraction of releases have no parity point at all---\kvcParityInfShareTwentyThree\% in 2023 versus \kvcParityInfShareTwentySix\% in 2026---because their caches are bounded (fully windowed or recurrent) and saturate below the weight bytes; we censor them to infinity and exclude them from the medians. Against the pre-registered claim, H4 is \kvcHFourVerdict{} on the pre-registered unit: pooled 2025--2026 median $T^{*}$ is \kvcHFourRatio{}$\times$ the 2023 median, far from the registered $10\times$. The verdict is unit-sensitive: under geometry deduplication the yearly medians (\kvcParityMedGeoTwentyThree, \kvcParityMedGeoTwentyFour, \kvcParityMedGeoTwentyFive, \kvcParityMedGeoTwentySix) give a pooled ratio of \kvcHFourRatioGeo$\times$, which crosses the registered threshold. The honest summary is that parity contexts grew several-fold to an order of magnitude depending on the counting unit---substantially, but not by the orders of magnitude the hypothesis claimed---while context demand grew apace \cite{peng2023yarn}.

The validation experiment instantiates reduced-depth, randomly initialized models from the snapshotted configs and measures the bytes of every cache tensor after prefill. It covers \kvcValRows{} models spanning \kvcValClasses{} attention classes (mha, gqa, windowed GQA hybrids, MLA, hybrid-Mamba, pure recurrent), and \kvcValWithin{} of the rows agree with the analytic accounting within 1\%, meeting the pre-registered success criteria (Figure~\ref{fig:validation}, Table~\ref{tab:validation}). Three implementation findings explain every nonzero gap. First, \texttt{transformers}~5.19 enforces sliding windows in the cache itself, not only in the attention mask: once a window saturates, the implementation truncates the stored tensor and retains one position fewer than the analytic $\min(T,W)$, which accounts for the small negative deviations on the two windowed probes. Second, the MLA implementation caches the compressed latent---the low-rank plus rope dimensions per token---and never materializes decompressed keys and values, so the theoretical latent accounting matches exactly \cite{deepseekai2024v2}. Third, recurrent layers pad their convolution state to the full kernel width, where the analytic model charges the theoretical minimum of stored inputs; this conv-state padding is the only deviation above 1\% (the pure-Mamba probe) and also explains the sub-1\% residual on the hybrid probe \cite{gu2023mamba}. The experiment runs in float32 and compares against float32 analytic bytes; real BF16 serving halves both sides of the comparison, so the relative agreement carries over but the absolute byte counts do not.
